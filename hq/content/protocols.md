## Get ready for focus

Run this at 10:15. The 10:00 Concerta dose is climbing toward peak — you're staging the launch on the pharmacological upslope, so the hardest minute of the day lands while the drug does the lifting.

1. Phone into the Mindsight box, locked until 15:15 — Barkley's rule: ADHD fails at the point of performance, so fix the environment, not the intention.
2. Loops in, Sonos Ace over them, ANC on — every ambient sound is a bid on working memory you can't afford; close the auction.
3. Full water bottle on the desk — Concerta mutes thirst signals, and mild dehydration degrades attention before you feel it.
4. Cube timer flipped and running where you can see it — the countdown starts before you do.
5. One window: the Flow task. Now card visible on the second monitor. Everything else closed — each open tab is a pre-loaded exit, and you don't get to pre-load exits.
6. Two brisk minutes on the treadmill or stairs, or ten slow breaths — movement bumps norepinephrine and dopamine, the exact chemistry the task needs, at cardiac-safe volume.
7. Sit, open the file, produce the first ugly unit — one bad sentence, one failing test — within 60 seconds. Motivation follows action, never the reverse; starting is the entire boss fight.

> Same order, every day. The sequence itself becomes the cue — by step 3 your brain already knows what's coming.

## Anti-rut protocol

For the mid-peak stall: 12:40, twelve minutes staring at the same paragraph. Don't push through — pushing through spends norepinephrine you demonstrably don't have right now. Reset instead.

1. Stand up. Immediately, mid-sentence — the freeze is postural before it's mental, and breaking the body's position breaks the loop's grip.
2. Five minutes outside in daylight, no phone — light plus gait restores norepinephrine; the phone would swap the walk's slow dopamine for cheap dopamine and void the reset.
3. Cold water on face and wrists — kicks the dive response, drops sympathetic noise without touching your stimulant load.
4. One full glass of water — see step 3 of the launch sequence; you're behind on it by now.
5. Return and shrink the task to a 10-minute version — you stalled because the full task went blurry; working memory can hold a 10-minute shape.

> Still stalled after two resets? The task is wrong-sized or wrong-classified. Demote it and pull the next card. That's the system correcting itself, not you failing.

## The coherence ritual (15:30)

Fifteen minutes at 15:30 sharp — deliberately inside the last med window. Organizing is executive work: sorting, deciding, and prioritizing burn the same prefrontal fuel as writing. Do it medicated at 15:30, or do it badly at 21:00 with nothing left in the tank.

Open today's Daybook page. The agent has already made a pass at the day's brain dump — every line proposed as a Project action, a Library resource, a Someday, or a drop. Your job is not to sort. Your job is four moves:

1. Veto the bad sorts — tick the checkbox on anything filed wrong. Veto is recognition, not generation, and recognition is an order of magnitude cheaper for a tired prefrontal cortex.
2. Confirm tomorrow's #1 Flow card — the decision gets made today, medicated, so tomorrow's ramp starts with zero decisions in it.
3. Glance at the map toggle — thirty seconds against the north stars, so the days keep pointing somewhere instead of just accumulating.
4. Close the laptop. That's the whole gesture — an explicit ending closes the open loops that would otherwise poll your attention all evening.

> Missed it? Agents file everything best-effort at 20:00 and tomorrow still works. No catch-up, ever — catch-up debt is how ADHD systems die, so this one doesn't issue any.

## Shutdown & consumption rules

- Podcasts and Substacks only after the ritual, only from the Queue — consumption you scheduled is rest; consumption you stole is guilt with audio, and guilt makes rest non-restorative.
- Anything tempting during the day gets one motion: into the Queue, consumed tonight — capture beats willpower because it costs one action instead of a standing act of resistance.
- No work inputs after shutdown. No "quick look" at the doc, no idea-adjacent YouTube — every evening work input reopens loops and borrows against tomorrow's dopamine baseline.
- Morning no-scroll: the first input of the day is the Today callout, not a feed — a feed is a variable-reward slot machine, and ten minutes of it recalibrates your reward baseline so every light task on the ramp reads gray.

## Focus setup (the physical layer)

- The desk is for deep work only — brains condition on context, and a desk where you scroll becomes a desk where you scroll.
- Mindsight box is the default state during peak, not the exception — the phone's cost isn't the checking, it's the standing awareness that you could.
- Loops + Sonos Ace stacked is the entry uniform for flow — for a noise-hypersensitive brain, silence isn't comfort, it's reclaimed working memory.
- Cube timer running in view — ADHD time-blindness means digits don't register; a physical object visibly running does.
- Whiteboard = parking lot. Intrusive thought mid-peak: write it, return to the task — once captured, the loop closes and working memory lets go; unwritten, it keeps polling.
- The Rail lives on its own monitor/Space — for ADHD, out of sight is out of existence; the Now card stays in the visual field or it stops being now.
