# The Last 10% Tax

## I shipped half of what I built. The runway was never the problem.

It's Friday at 7pm. The oracle-problem essay has been "content-ready" since Monday morning — drafted, reviewed, in voice, ready. It's still sitting in the editor. The publish button is two pixels and a confirm dialog. I haven't pressed it.

Meanwhile this month: pivoted Lifebet to Keep. Wired Whoop OAuth in production. Pushed Pickless live with its own MCP package. Migrated five apps to Gemini in one Thursday session. Drafted the YC application, sharpened it, sat on it. Built BONP, a compaction predictor that runs on every tool call, an arxiv digest agent, a contra.ventures studio site with nineteen sections. Shipped param-hub v2 in Japanese-Nordic. Cleared 21% of disk space and put memory on git with hourly auto-commits.

Nothing on that list took more than a long afternoon. Most took a single session. The throughput wasn't the issue.

The issue was the last 10%. The git push that's been sitting since the 19th. The Anthropic Fellows application that evaporated despite two weeks of runway. The Substack and LinkedIn posts that have been ready since week one and are now fifteen days overdue. Three real-money applications in a month that closed without a public-facing artifact. The work was done. The publish wasn't.

Whoop tells the same story. April average recovery: 46%. Best day was the 22nd at 92%, then I burned it on a single Thursday session and the next three days came back 19, 17, 17. Two back-to-back red days with zero protocol adjustment. The data was there. Nothing in the schedule changed. The biometric alerts fire into a body that doesn't act on them.

The pattern is structural. In April, the cost of building was near-zero. The Claude harness is so good that any feature that would have taken a week six months ago now takes ninety minutes. You can build five MCP servers in a Sunday. You can ship Pickless from concept to live URL between meals. The capacity constraint that defined the previous decade of solo founders has been quietly removed.

What replaced it is a new tax: the last 10%. The publish, the push, the commit-to-public, the registrar-credit-card, the email-the-investor, the click-confirm-deploy. Every one of those is roughly the same friction it was in 2020 — a five-minute action that requires presenting a finished thing to the world. And when capacity is unlimited, that five-minute action gets crowded out by the next build. Shipping velocity becomes shipping cosplay.

The Lifebet → Keep pivot is the one thing this month that did close. I spent a day naming it, registered the angle — charity-forfeit instead of for-profit prediction markets, oracle-as-moat — and said it out loud to enough people that it became real. That's why it stuck. Once you say "Keep" out loud to a room, you can't quietly slide back to Lifebet a week later.

Everything else I built this month is sitting one publish-button-press from being real. Including the essay you're reading.

So the next 30 days is not a build month. It's a publish month. The Substack goes up Sunday. LinkedIn Monday. Keep YC submission by May 4, no more sharpening passes — the version on disk is the version that goes in. Anthropic Fellows is dead; I learned from that one. The git pushes happen at end of session, not at end of project. And recovery red days mean schedule changes, not better dashboards.

April proved I can build anything in ninety minutes. The next 30 days have to prove I can finish.
